An urban UAM operator evaluates capacity and financial performance over a typical one-hour passenger peak. Its network consists of endpoint vertiports A and B and transfer-hub vertiport H. All three vertiports remain open. The operator must install `n_A,n_H,n_B in {1,2,3}` pads and select one common passenger fare `p in {55,70,85}` dollars. Hourly pad costs are `K_A(1)=60`, `K_A(2)=180`, `K_A(3)=420`; `K_H(1)=90`, `K_H(2)=190`, `K_H(3)=360`; and `K_B(1)=70`, `K_B(2)=200`, `K_B(3)=440` dollars.

The six origin–destination demand groups are specified by `(physical UAM itinerary, potential hourly demand, ground generalized travel time in minutes, pre-wait UAM generalized travel time in minutes, per-UAM-passenger operating cost in dollars)`: `M1=(A-H-B,7,42,16,30)`, `M2=(B-H-A,5,38,15,30)`, `M3=(A-H,4,24,8,18)`, `M4=(H-B,6,25,9,18)`, `M5=(B-H,3,21,8,18)`, and `M6=(H-A,2.5,22,7,18)`. For demand group `i`, the ground and UAM choice weights are `exp(-0.18*g_i)` and `exp[-0.035*p-0.18*T_i]`, respectively. Expected UAM passenger demand may be fractional.

Each takeoff or landing generates one pad-service request, meaning one aircraft-movement request handled by a pad at the corresponding vertiport. All pad-service requests at a vertiport join the same first-come, first-served queue. A direct A-H, H-A, H-B, or B-H passenger generates one request at the origin and one at the destination. An A-H-B passenger generates four requests: takeoff at A, landing at H, takeoff at H after transfer, and landing at B. The reverse itinerary generates the analogous four requests. A transfer passenger therefore generates two separate pad-service requests at H. The listed pre-wait UAM time already includes flight and transfer-processing time but excludes all pad waiting times. The value `T_i` is obtained by adding the expected wait for every pad-service request in the passenger's physical itinerary.

Pad-service request arrivals are modeled as Poisson, pad-service times as deterministic, and the pads at a vertiport as homogeneous parallel servers with per-pad service rate `mu=12` requests per hour. For `n` pads and pad-service request arrival rate `lambda<n*mu`, use

`W_n(lambda)=30*C(n,lambda/mu)/(n*mu-lambda)` minutes,

where `a=lambda/mu`, `rho=lambda/(n*mu)`, and

`C(n,a)=[a^n/(n!*(1-rho))]/[sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a^k/k!+a^n/(n!*(1-rho))]`.

The waiting time displayed at a vertiport for the peak hour is generated by all takeoff and landing pad-service requests made there during that same hour. Passengers use those waiting times in their ground-versus-UAM choice weights. A fare-and-pad configuration is admissible only if the mutually consistent pad-service request arrival rate at every vertiport is strictly below the service capacity of its installed pads.

The planning file contains two legacy summaries. Its itinerary tab stores one row per distinct vertiport visited and displays H only once for an A-H-B or B-H-A booking. Its commercial forecast assumes zero pad waiting time and reports total expected UAM passenger counts of 21.828866, 19.423261, and 16.615518 at fares 55, 70, and 85. Audit those summaries against all physical pad-service events, passenger mode-choice relations, and vertiport service rules stated above. Then choose the common fare and pad counts that maximize expected peak-hour profit, defined as fare revenue minus per-UAM-passenger operating costs and pad costs.
